Inspiration
The topic of this project comes from my personal experience using short-form video platforms such as TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. While watching these platforms, I often hear the same viral songs repeatedly, especially the hook or chorus section used in dance challenges, memes, or trends. This made me wonder whether short-form platforms influence the music structure and diversity. Is it possible for the music producer or artist to create a song serving short-form video trend. To narrow down the scope, Tiktok is considered the most prominent of short clip and music challenge.

Methodology
📊
Audio Feature Comparison
Mean & SD of danceability, energy, valence, duration, tempo across eras
📉
Diversity Over Time
Yearly SD tracking to measure convergence of sonic features 2013–2022 and Shannon entropy of genre tags per year
🔄
Influence Cross-Platform
Lagged overlap counts TikTok ↔ Spotify and TikTok ↔ Billboard to identify influence direction
Key Finding
Although music features changed over time, there is limited evidence that TikTok directly caused these changes. Instead, TikTok appears to amplify songs that are already successful on platforms such as Spotify and Billboard, reinforcing existing trends rather than acting as a trend setter. While post-TikTok songs became shorter, more danceable, and more similar in their audio characteristics, the influence analysis suggests that music popularity on established platforms often precedes success on TikTok rather than the reverse.
